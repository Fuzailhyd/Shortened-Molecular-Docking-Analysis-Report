Molecular docking analysis report for cancer research
​Pre-eminent computational tools were utilized to evaluate the binding profile of the synthesized ligand within the receptor's active site.
​The 3D binding conformation (Figure 1) elucidates a highly optimized spatial orientation and structural fit inside the pocket.
​Structural analysis reveals a dense network of non-covalent interactions that effectively stabilize the core scaffold of the molecule.
​The 2D interaction map (Figure 2) highlights the specific amino acid residues governing the binding thermodynamics.
​Hydrogen bonding plays a crucial role, with water-mediated bridges (HOH 825, 828, 848) actively stabilizing the complex.
​These bridging water molecules facilitate crucial electrostatic connections between the ligand's polar groups and the receptor.
​A strong, direct hydrogen bond is observed between the ligand's carbonyl oxygen and the acidic residue ASP A:521.
​This specific interaction with ASP A:521 locks the conformation, preventing unwanted rotational flexibility of the scaffold.
​SER A:543 participates in a high-density interaction network, contributing significantly to the total binding free energy.
​The backbone atoms of GLY A:541 form intimate contacts, indicating a tight orientation within a conserved region.
​Hydrophobic interactions, particularly \pi-effects, further fortify the stability and alignment of the docked complex.
​TYR A:545 is engaged in a distinct \pi-interaction, adding a hydrophobic lock to the upper boundary of the binding site.
​Basic residues like ARG A:520 exhibit positive charge-based interactions that complement the electronic cloud of the ligand.
​Another critical residue, ARG A:544, shows extensive interaction lines, acting as a major electrostatic anchoring hub.
​The halogenated phenyl ring, featuring a fluorine substituent, extends deeper into a specialized hydrophobic sub-pocket.
​The presence of the fluorine atom enhances lipophilicity, allowing optimal packing and preventing ligand slippage.
​No significant unfavorable steric clashes or repulsive bumps were detected, confirming an energetically favorable relaxed geometry.
​The structural topology of the binding pocket appears to perfectly match the essential pharmacophoric features of the molecule.
​In conclusion, the molecular docking simulation successfully validates the highly competitive inhibitory potential of the compound.
​This robust interaction profile strongly justifies pushing this candidate forward into advanced molecular dynamics (MD) simulations
